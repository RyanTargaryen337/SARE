# Gate 0 vendor interview script

**Goal:** find out whether late or unreliable payouts are a real, frequent and costly problem for food vendors,
and get 5 vendors to commit to a Sàrè pilot (Gate 0, `docs/designs/sare-platform.md`).

**Length:** 20 to 30 minutes. In person if possible: at the shop, outside rush hours.

**Rules for the interviewer**

- **Ask about what already happened, not what they'd do.** "When did you last wait for a payout?" beats
  "Would you like faster payouts?" Everyone says yes to the second question.
- **Don't pitch until Part 6.** If you describe Sàrè early, they'll tell you what you want to hear.
- **Ask for numbers and dates:** how many days, how much money, which app, last week or last month.
- **Follow the pain.** When they get animated, ask "Tell me more about that" and "What happened next?"
- **Listen more than you talk:** about 80% them, 20% you.
- **Use their language.** Switch to Pidgin, Yoruba, Igbo or Hausa when that's more natural.
- **Ask permission before recording** ("Can I record so I don't miss anything? It stays with me.").
  Don't record or write down bank details, BVN or NIN. You don't need them, and Nigeria's data protection law
  (the NDPA) applies.

## Part 1: Opening (2 min)

> "Thanks for your time. I'm [name]. I'm talking to food vendors in [city] about how they sell and how they
> get paid. I'm not selling anything today. I want to understand how your business works. There are no wrong
> answers, and if something is private, just skip it."

## Part 2: The business (3 min)

1. What do you sell, and how long have you been doing it?
2. On a normal day, roughly how many orders do you get? What's a typical order worth?
3. How many people work with you?

## Part 3: How orders come in (5 min)

4. Walk me through the last order you got that wasn't a walk-in. Where did it come from?
   *(WhatsApp, phone call, Instagram, Chowdeck, Glovo, other)*
5. Out of 10 orders, how many come from each of those?
6. **If they use a delivery app:**
   - Which ones? Since when?
   - What percentage do they take per order? *(Get the exact figure, or ask them to check the app.)*
   - Have you raised your prices on the app compared with your shop? By how much, and why?
   - What made you join? What would make you leave?
7. **If they don't use a delivery app:** Why not? *(Not available here, too expensive, tried it and stopped…)*

## Part 4: How money reaches them (8 min). This is the core.

8. When a customer pays by transfer or through an app, when does that money actually land in your account?
   Tell me about the last time.
9. What's the longest you've waited for money you were owed? What happened?
10. How often does a payout arrive late, short or not at all? *(per week or per month)*
11. When money is late, what does it affect? *(buying stock, paying staff, rent, borrowing)*
    Have you ever borrowed or delayed paying someone because a payout was late?
12. How do you keep track of who has paid and who hasn't? How much time does that take each day?
13. Have you had problems with customer transfers: fake alerts, reversals, transfers that never arrive?
14. If you could change one thing about how you get paid, what would it be?

## Part 5: Delivery (3 min)

15. Who delivers your orders today: your own rider, a dispatch rider you call, the app, or the customer picks up?
16. What do you charge for delivery? Who sets the price?
17. If you had more orders tomorrow, could you deliver them?

## Part 6: Short pitch and reaction (5 min)

Only now, describe Sàrè in plain words:

> "Here's what I'm building. You get your own ordering link to share with your customers on WhatsApp or Instagram.
> Customers pay by card, transfer or USSD through Paystack or Flutterwave. The payment is split automatically:
> your share goes straight to your bank account the next business day, and Sàrè never holds your money. Sàrè takes
> 5% of the food bill, with a minimum of ₦200 per order, and nothing on your delivery fee when your own rider
> delivers. Customers see a ₦100 service fee on the menu. You set your own menu prices and delivery fee. No monthly
> fee, no signup fee."

18. What's your first reaction? What sounds good, and what worries you?
19. Is 5% of the food bill (₦200 minimum) fair to you? What would make it not worth it?
20. What would you need to see before you trust it with your orders?

## Part 7: The commitment ask (2 min)

Gate 0 needs real commitments, not compliments. Ask directly:

> "I'm starting a small pilot with 5 vendors in [city] in [month]. Would you be one of them? It means using the
> link for your orders for [4 weeks] and telling me honestly what works and what doesn't."

- **Yes:** agree on a start date and the best way to reach them. Ask: "Who else should I talk to? Can you
  introduce me?"
- **Maybe:** ask "What would need to be true for this to be a yes?" Write down the exact answer.
- **No:** ask "What's the main reason?" Thank them and ask for one referral.

## Close

> "Thank you, this was really helpful. Can I come back to you with questions as I build?"

## What to report back

After every 5 interviews, add up:

- How many vendors named payout timing as their #1 money problem.
- The median usual wait and the median longest wait, in days.
- How many said yes, maybe and no to the pilot. A "maybe" doesn't count toward Gate 0.
- The 2 or 3 strongest quotes, word for word (with permission).

These numbers go into the investor deck, the a16z application and the Gate 0 decision. A suggested rule of
thumb (not yet in the plan): if fewer than half of the vendors you interview name payouts as a real problem,
stop and rethink the wedge before building.
