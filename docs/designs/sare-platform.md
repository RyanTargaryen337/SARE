## Problem Statement

Nigerian food vendors depend on delivery marketplaces (mainly Chowdeck, about 1.5M customers in 11 cities) that take 20 to 30% commission, are publicly accused of marking up menu prices 25 to 50% (consumer suit at the FCCPC tribunal, Feb 2026), and, per the founder's own vendor conversations, pay vendors late. Customers outside a handful of cities have no delivery at all. Sàrè is a three-sided marketplace (vendors, riders, customers) built to be trusted on money: vendors paid automatically by the next business day with no holds, prices shown without hidden markup, and verification concentrated where money leaves the platform.

## Demand Evidence

- No one depends on Sàrè yet. Evidence today is desk research plus "a few" vendor conversations (no names recorded).
- Asked which complaint came up first or loudest without prompting, the founder answered: **"Payout delays."**
- A web search found no public reporting of Chowdeck paying vendors late; public complaints are customer-side (markups, slow deliveries, refunds). The payout pain is therefore unverified outside the founder's conversations. Confirming it is Gate 0, which must pass before any Phase 1 code is written (see Recommended Approach and The Assignment).

## Status Quo

Vendors list on Chowdeck/Glovo, absorb 20 to 30% commission, raise app prices to compensate, and wait on payouts. Many also keep WhatsApp regulars who now get routed through the marketplace, where the vendor pays commission on a customer they already had. Bolt Food and Jumia Food left Nigeria (Dec 2023); FoodCourt and GoLemon shut down after owning kitchens and warehouses. Asset-light marketplaces survived. HeyFood grew by avoiding Lagos (Ibadan, Abuja, Benin) and placing agents inside restaurants.

## Target User & Narrowest Wedge

- **First customer:** the food vendor who already sells on an incumbent and is hurt by late payouts. Not yet a named person; naming five is the Assignment.
- **Wedge:** "paid automatically, every business day, no holds and no withdrawal requests." Each vendor gets an order link for their own regulars, delivers themselves if Sàrè has no riders nearby, and the processor settles the vendor's share straight to their bank on its standard schedule. The committed promise is **next business day** (the usual T+1 for processor split settlement). A same-night promise is only made if Gate 0 confirms a processor mechanism that delivers it without Sàrè holding funds. The same promise applies to every vendor tier. This works in all 36 states plus the FCT on day one because it needs neither Sàrè customers nor Sàrè riders.

## Constraints

- Pre-product, no revenue. Existing code is a browser-only simulation (React 19, Vite, Tailwind, shadcn/ui) with no backend.
- Sàrè must never hold customer funds in any Sàrè-controlled account, wallet or rider float. All card, transfer and USSD collection runs through a licensed processor's split settlement (Paystack or Flutterwave subaccounts), which avoids CBN licensing and float risk. Physical cash is only ever handled by the vendor or the vendor's own rider, never by Sàrè or a Sàrè rider (see Phase 2 and Phase 3). Sàrè's own money (company capital) is separate and funds the refund reserve described in Phase 1.
- Customers verify by phone OTP only. No NIN or BVN is collected from customers (founder decision). Vendors and riders are strongly verified.
- NDPA 2023 applies from the first vendor BVN/NIN collected: consent, 30-day data-subject requests, 72-hour breach notice, DPO before launch.
- Lagos riders need LASDRI registration; NHIA health insurance enrolment is required for platform riders.
- Nigeria's mobile reality: low-end Android, expensive data, 36% mobile internet adoption. The web app must be light; USSD and WhatsApp channels follow.

2. v1 is a nationwide vendor tool: per-vendor order links for their own customers, next-business-day settlement (same-night only if Gate 0 confirms a way), vendor self-delivery where Sàrè has no riders. Sàrè's marketplace and dispatch open zone by zone where vendor, rider and customer density exists. (revised during the session from "one city first", after the second opinion showed a nationwide signup with no orders yields a fast payout of ₦0)
3. Asset-light: existing kitchens and independent riders. No owned kitchens, warehouses or fleet. Sàrè never holds funds. (agreed)
4. The research memo is the roadmap. The build is phased so each phase ships to real users. (agreed; the founder then chose to commit to the full roadmap, see Recommended Approach)
5. Payout pain gets confirmed unprompted with 5+ vendors, with at least one late or stuck payout shown, before any Phase 1 code is written (Gate 0). (agreed; timing fixed in review)

## Approaches Considered

- **A: Concierge test.** Manual Paystack split links for 5 vendors' WhatsApp regulars, 2 weeks, spreadsheet ledger. Recommended by the facilitator; not chosen, because the founder wants to build the platform now.
- **B: Vendor order-link app.** The 48-hour prototype above. Not chosen as the end state; it becomes Phase 1 of the chosen approach.
- **C: Payout advance on incumbent orders.** Pay vendors tonight for Chowdeck orders, collect later. Rejected: needs a lending licence and capital, and depends on settlement data Sàrè doesn't control.
- **D: Full platform per the research memo.** Chosen.

## Recommended Approach

**D: the full platform, built in phases with evidence gates.** The founder chose the full roadmap over the facilitator's recommendation of A. To keep that choice from becoming the FoodCourt path (building 24 answers before confirming one question), the phases are ordered so the payout thesis meets real vendors first and the expensive pieces (dispatch, USSD, languages, rider compliance) only start after a gate passes.

**Gate 0 — before any Phase 1 code.** This is The Assignment. Pass condition: 5+ named vendors raised payout delays without being prompted, at least one showed a late or stuck incumbent payout, and 5 agreed to run a week of their own orders through a manually set-up processor payment link. In the same week, confirm with Paystack and Flutterwave (a) the actual subaccount settlement timing including weekends and public holidays, (b) how refunds and chargebacks on split payments are debited, (c) whether a per-subaccount settlement schedule exists, (d) whether per-transaction split amounts are supported (if not, vendor debt is recovered only by vendor transfer, with the debt cap enforced), (e) transfer and USSD fee pricing, and (f) the SMS provider's per-message cost to Nigerian numbers (if above ₦5, re-derive the minimum commission so the thinnest order stays positive under the CEO-M1 margin rule). If Gate 0 fails, stop and revisit the wedge before building.

**Phase 1 — Vendor payouts (all 36 states + FCT).** Ships the wedge.
- Backend: Postgres (Supabase) with state → LGA → area/landmark text from day one. (LGA classification and the NIPOST postcode field move to Phase 2, CEO-D1.)
- Vendor onboarding, tiered. Every tier gets the same settlement timing (the processor's schedule); tiers only change the weekly sales cap, which Sàrè enforces by refusing new checkouts once a vendor reaches it, never by holding or delaying money:
  - Tier 1 (unregistered): phone OTP + BVN + bank account-name match. Weekly sales cap (starting value ₦150,000, set in config).
  - Tier 2 (registered): adds CAC lookup + TIN. Higher cap (starting value ₦1,000,000, reset from Gate 0 vendor volumes to at least 2× the largest Gate 0 vendor's weekly sales; a vendor nearing the cap can ask the founder to raise it).
  - Tier 3 (trusted): moved to Phase 2 (CEO-D2), together with the priority placement it unlocks.
  - Impersonation guard: moved to Phase 2 (CEO-D3), when vendor listings become publicly searchable.
- Revenue (Phase 1): the customer pays the vendor's menu price plus the vendor's delivery fee, nothing else. Sàrè's fee is a commission deducted from the vendor's share inside the split: 5% of the full amount charged (food plus the vendor's delivery fee, since the processor fee is charged on the full amount), with a minimum of ₦200 per order on every payment method (raised from ₦150 by CEO-M1 so messaging costs are covered). Starting rate; confirmed or changed during Gate 0. Margin rule: commission ≥ processor fee + per-order messaging cost (about 5 SMS per order at an estimated ₦5 each, about ₦25, confirmed with the SMS provider in Gate 0). Sàrè bears the processor's transaction fee out of its commission (split "bearer" set to the main account), so the vendor's net is exactly total minus the commission. Check at published local card pricing of about 1.5% + ₦100 (₦100 waived under ₦2,500, capped at ₦2,000), with about ₦25 of SMS per order: ₦2,000 → ₦200 commission vs ₦30 fee + ₦25 SMS; ₦3,000 → ₦200 vs ₦145 + ₦25 (+₦30); ₦4,000 → ₦200 vs ₦160 + ₦25 (+₦15, the thinnest point); ₦5,000 → ₦250 vs ₦175 + ₦25; ₦50,000 → ₦2,500 vs ₦850 + ₦25. The daily vendor payout SMS (CEO-E1) is a per-vendor cost, covered by commission once a vendor averages more than one order per settled day; it is tracked in the Gate 1 unit-economics check. Transfer and USSD fees are checked against the same rule in Gate 0. On refunded orders Sàrè keeps no commission and the processor fee is usually not returned: if the refund is the vendor's fault (rejection after payment, timeout, non-delivery, wrong order) the fee is added to the vendor's negative balance; if the customer cancels before acceptance, Sàrè absorbs it, which the Phase 2 refund-rate target (under 3%) keeps small. The customer receipt shows it as "Sàrè fee (paid by vendor)" so prices are never inflated to hide it. Marketplace orders from Phase 2 use the 25/20/15% tiers.
- Menu with the vendor's own prices.
- Order lifecycle:
  1. Customer opens the vendor's order link, verifies by phone OTP, enters a delivery address (state, LGA, area, landmark text, optional Plus Code) and pays by card, transfer or USSD through the processor.
  2. Vendor is notified by SMS and web push, with a WhatsApp click-to-chat link to the customer. Vendor accepts or rejects in the web dashboard and, on accepting, quotes a delivery ETA (default 60 minutes, maximum 180). No answer in 10 minutes auto-cancels and refunds.
  3. Delivery fee: the vendor sets a flat fee per LGA they serve, or "pickup only". The fee goes to the vendor in full.
  4. Customer gets an order status page (link) plus SMS on accepted, out for delivery and delivered. The vendor taps each status; "delivered" can also be confirmed by the customer.
- Refunds, cancellations and disputes (Phase 1):
  - Customer can cancel for a full refund until the vendor accepts.
  - Vendor rejection or the 10-minute timeout refunds in full automatically.
  - Stuck orders: if an accepted order is not marked delivered by its ETA + 60 minutes, the customer's status page shows a "Not delivered" button and the vendor gets an SMS. At ETA + 24 hours with no delivered mark, the order is refunded automatically.
  - "Not delivered" reports open at whichever comes first, the delivered mark or ETA + 60 minutes, and close 2 hours after whichever comes later, the delivered mark or the ETA. A delivered mark tapped only by the vendor does not cancel the stuck-order SMS or the ETA + 24 hour auto-refund until the customer confirms delivery or the report window closes without a report. "Wrong order" reports open at the delivered mark and close 2 hours after it. The vendor has 24 hours to respond with evidence; with no response or no evidence, the customer is refunded.
  - Refunds and chargebacks are borne by the vendor's share (Sàrè's fee on that order is refunded too). Because disputes can close after T+1 settlement has already paid the vendor, the processor will often debit Sàrè's main account. Negative-balance policy:
    - **Refund reserve:** Sàrè keeps a reserve of its own capital in the main account, starting at ₦500,000 and topped up to that level weekly. Refunds are paid from it; if it cannot cover a refund, new checkouts pause platform-wide and the founder is alerted.
    - **Recovery:** the amount becomes a negative balance on the vendor ledger and is recovered by raising Sàrè's per-transaction split share on that vendor's next orders (per-transaction split amounts, confirmed in Gate 0) until it is cleared.
    - **Cap:** a vendor's outstanding debt (refunds plus unpaid cash-order commission) may not exceed ₦20,000 or 3 days of that vendor's average sales, whichever is lower. Above the cap, new checkouts and cash orders for that vendor are blocked until it is cleared by transfer or recovered from orders.
    - **Write-off:** debt unrecovered after 60 days, or on vendor churn, is absorbed by Sàrè and written off, and the vendor account is suspended.
- Settlement: processor split payments, vendor share straight to their bank. Sàrè never touches the funds.
- Vendor payout ledger: every order, fee, refund and settlement time.
- Morning payout message (CEO-E1), SMS only in Phase 1 (WhatsApp messaging stays in Phase 4): each business day, after the nightly reconciliation and the 09:00 "Late" check have run, every vendor with a settlement in the previous processor batch gets one SMS: "<Processor> sent ₦X for N orders to <bank> ••1234 on <date>. Next settlement: <next business day>." The wording says "sent", never "landed", because processor reports confirm the payout was sent, not when the bank credited it. Orders already flagged "Late" are excluded and the SMS adds "M orders late, we're on it". No message on days without a settlement (weekends, public holidays, no orders). A failed send is retried once; the ledger always shows the same information. Vendors can switch the message off in the dashboard. The "Late" alert SMS itself is the existing missing-settlement step, not part of this message.
- Payout-delay tracker (CEO-E2): optional fields where a vendor logs, per sales day, the date an incumbent platform paid them for that day. The ledger shows "days gained" = average over the last 30 days of (incumbent payout date − sales date) − (Sàrè payout sent date − sales date), counted in calendar days, over days that have both values. It is labelled "based on dates you entered" and is for the vendor's own view and the founder's internal evidence only, never for public claims about a named competitor.
- In-store price badge (CEO-E3, report rules CEO-E3a): at menu setup, and again on any menu price change, the vendor attests that order-link prices equal in-store prices; the order page shows the badge. Only an OTP-verified customer with a delivered order from that vendor can report "price differs from in-store", at most once per order. Reports from two different customers within 30 days hide the badge and SMS the vendor, who can restore it by uploading a photo of their menu board within 48 hours; the founder compares it with the order-link menu within 2 business days. No photo, or a mismatch, keeps the badge off for 30 days, after which it returns automatically. Restoring the badge, or a fresh attestation after a price change, resets the report count to zero.
- Order page performance budget (CEO-E4, approach CEO-E4a): the customer order page and status page are small pre-built static pages without React that fetch the vendor's menu and order status as JSON. Payment redirects to the processor's hosted checkout page, which is outside the budget and keeps card data off Sàrè's pages. Budget: under 150 KB gzipped on first load per page, counting HTML, CSS, JS, fonts, the menu JSON and any images that load before scroll; menu item photos are lazy-loaded below the fold and capped at 30 KB each. Critical path: 50 KB gzipped or less (HTML, inline CSS, JS and menu JSON) must be enough to draw the menu text; everything else is deferred. Targets: menu text readable within 5 seconds on a 2G profile (250 kbps, 300 ms latency). CI runs two checks: a size check that fails above 150 KB total or 50 KB critical path, and a throttled Lighthouse run with that 2G profile that fails if the menu text takes longer than 5 seconds. The vendor dashboard keeps the React/shadcn stack.
- Missing settlement handling: a nightly job reconciles orders against processor settlement reports. At 09:00 the next business day after a settlement was due, unmatched orders show as "Late" on the vendor's ledger and the vendor gets an SMS. The ops owner (the founder in Phase 1) is alerted by SMS and email, opens a processor ticket the same day, and the target resolution is 2 business days, with an SMS update to the vendor at each step.
- NDPA basics: granular consent screens, data export/delete request flow, named DPO. Raw BVN and NIN numbers are never stored by Sàrè: they are sent to the KYC provider, and Sàrè stores only the verification result, the provider reference and the last 4 digits. Verification results are kept while the vendor account is active plus 6 years for audit, then deleted. The database is hosted in the nearest available region (Supabase has no Nigerian region), with NDPA cross-border transfer covered by the provider's data processing agreement and standard contractual safeguards, recorded in the privacy notice.
- **Gate 1 (after Phase 1, before Phase 2):** 20+ vendors processing real orders weekly through their links, at least 95% of orders settled on the committed day, and the nationwide falsification test: the cohort is the first 10 vendors to sign up outside Lagos, Abuja, Port Harcourt and Ibadan (the candidate Phase 2 cities, fixed before Phase 1 launch), and the cohort closes at the 10th such signup. At least 3 must still take orders through Sàrè in the fourth week after their own first order. If that test fails, nationwide signup is wrong: stop open signup outside planned zones and recruit vendors zone by zone.

**Phase 2 — Marketplace and customer trust (first dense zone).**
- Pick the first zone from Phase 1 data (where vendors and orders cluster), not by assumption.
- Moved from Phase 1 by the CEO review: LGA classification (urban, semi-urban, rural, riverine) and NIPOST postcode field (CEO-D1); Tier 3 vendor verification (address check by geotagged photo or field visit + 30 clean orders, no sales cap, priority placement) (CEO-D2); impersonation guard with a defined brand list, match rule and review SLA (CEO-D3).
- Customer browse/search across vendors in the zone; transparent checkout (food → delivery → service fee → total).
- Customer trust tiers: new = prepaid only; established (4+ orders, <5% cancellations) = cash on delivery up to ₦10,000; trusted (10+ orders, <2%, no disputes) = up to ₦30,000. Cash on delivery is only offered when the vendor's own rider delivers; the cash goes to the vendor and never passes through Sàrè. Sàrè's commission on cash orders is recovered through the negative-balance policy in Phase 1 and counts toward the same per-vendor debt cap. Device binding, 90-day dormancy reset, address-level blacklisting, vendor flags feeding a shared reputation score.
- Cancellation windows: full refund before vendor acceptance, partial before prep, none after; one-click full refund for confirmed app faults. Duplicate-order guard (same basket within 90 seconds).
- **Gate 2 (before Phase 3):** in the zone, 30%+ of customers order again within 30 days and the refund rate is under 3% (the Success Criteria figures; provisional, and may be reset with Phase 1 data before Phase 2 starts).

**Phase 3 — Riders and dispatch (zone by zone).**
- Rider onboarding: NIN + BVN (NIBSS), FRSC licence check, vehicle papers + insurance, liveness face match, LASDRI in Lagos, background/watchlist screen, re-screen every 6 months.
- Dispatch: nearest-rider matching (the demo's simulation logic is the reference), predictive assignment from vendor prep-time history, handoff timer escalation.
- Rider protections: earnings breakdown per drop, peak-hour minimum per delivery, 6-hour continuous-work lockout, speed monitoring, accident fund contribution per delivery, NHIA enrolment.
- Tamper evidence: sealed bag with order seal code, photo at pickup and drop.
- No physical cash on Sàrè-rider deliveries (a rider holding customer cash would be a rider float, which the constraint forbids). Instead, established and trusted customers may choose "pay at the door": the rider shows a processor payment request (transfer, USSD or card) at handoff and releases the food once the processor confirms payment, so the money still settles through the split. If the customer refuses or is absent, the food returns to the vendor and Sàrè pays the vendor the food price from the refund reserve; the customer drops to prepaid-only and the delivery address is flagged.
- **Gate 3 (before Phase 4):** median door-to-door under 40 minutes in served zones, zero unverified riders active, and every order gross-margin positive before rider payout for 8 consecutive weeks.

**Phase 4 — Reach.**
- WhatsApp ordering (same order queue), then USSD for feature phones.
- Hausa, Yoruba, Igbo and Pidgin, with voice guidance; screen-reader pass (VoiceOver, TalkBack); low-end device and slow-network testing.
- In-app live chat, WhatsApp support, Nigerian phone line, escalation paths; automated refund for SLA breaches.
- Quarterly transparency report (vendors verified/rejected, impersonation cases, rider incidents, refund times).

Existing code reuse: the demo's Vendor Hub, Rider app and Ops views (`src/pages/*`) become the UI starting points; the demo's Customer page is a design reference only, because the customer order page is rebuilt as a static page under the CEO-E4 budget; `src/lib/data.ts` pricing constants become server-side config; `src/lib/sim.tsx` stays as the demo/simulation mode and the reference for dispatch logic.

### R3-1 — feasibility (blocking)

**Problem**

> Persisting R2-4 (margin part). The design now sets Sàrè as the fee bearer, but its proof that the 5% commission stays margin-positive is wrong. Using the document's own pricing (1.5% + ₦100, with the ₦100 waived under ₦2,500), a ₦2,500 food order earns ₦125 commission but pays a ₦137.50 fee. Break-even is about ₦2,857, so every card order from ₦2,500 to about ₦2,857 loses money. The processor fee is also charged on the whole transaction, including the vendor's delivery fee, while commission is 5% of the food subtotal only. For example, ₦2,000 food plus a ₦600 delivery fee earns ₦100 but pays ₦139. The ₦150 minimum commission is said to apply only where 'any method's fee exceeds 5%', and the text implies card does not qualify, yet it does. Refunds are still not costed: Sàrè refunds its own fee on refunded orders, while processor fees are typically not returned, so each refunded order is a net loss to Sàrè.

**Remedy**

> Redo the margin check on the full charged amount (food plus delivery fee) and on refunds. Then fix the model so every order is non-negative after processor fees: for example, a per-order minimum commission (₦150 or more) on all methods, including card, or commission on the full transaction. Also state whether Sàrè keeps the processor fee or its commission on refunded orders. Remove the 'positive on every basket size' claim unless the corrected model supports it.
